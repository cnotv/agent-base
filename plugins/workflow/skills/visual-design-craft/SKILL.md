# Visual Design Craft

A self-contained guide. Work through the parts that apply: brief, style, system, layout, illustration, production, review.

## 1. Brief

Write it in one line: audience, purpose, medium, 3-5 mood words, constraints (print or screen, size, budget, deadline). Ask one question only if audience or medium is missing; otherwise state assumptions and continue.

## 2. Style direction

Build directions from five axes and combine them:

- **Era or movement:** modernist grid, geometric abstraction, art deco, mid-century, postmodern layered, brutalist, retro-futurist, arts-and-crafts, minimalist, maximalist, editorial.
- **Medium feel:** watercolor, gouache, ink, risograph, linocut, collage, paper-cut, screenprint, photographic, clay 3D, flat vector, pixel.
- **Type voice:** geometric sans, grotesque, humanist serif, high-contrast serif, slab, hand-lettered, monospace.
- **Colour mood:** earthy, pastel, primary contrast, neon, monochrome plus one accent, dusk gradients.
- **Composition:** strict grid, asymmetric, centred, layered collage, full-bleed, white space, dense pattern.

Propose three directions: safe (one era plus one medium), blended (two influences), bold (unexpected pairing). For each give name, tone, palette, type, texture, layout rule, and where it could fail. Score each 1-5 on audience fit, legibility, production cost, longevity and originality; recommend one in two sentences. Adapt mechanics only; never copy a specific artwork, brand, character or artist's signature look.

## 3. Visual system (style kit)

- **Palette:** 6-10 named colours with hex, roles (background, text, accent, highlight, shadow), max 4 per layout. Contrast: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text.
- **Type:** two families at most. Size scale (e.g. 12/16/24/34/48), leading about 140% for body, line length 35-75 characters depending on medium, deep warm ink instead of pure black on cream.
- **Surface and texture:** one base paper or background tone, one grain reused everywhere as a low-opacity multiply layer, soft translucent washes, consistent edge treatment. Add the same grain over flat digital shapes so styles do not clash. Keep texture off small text.
- **Imagery rules:** subject, perspective, detail level, colour limits, photo treatment (crop, tint, grain).
- **Tokens:** margins, gutters, spacing scale (8-unit steps), corner radius, line weights.
- **Templates:** named frames and placeholders for each format used.

## 4. Layout

1. Confirm format, size, orientation, bleed (3 mm print), safe area, resolution (300 dpi print).
2. Set the grid: margins (inner larger than outer for bound pages), columns, baseline.
3. Decide the focal point, then 3-4 text levels by size, weight and colour. Group related items; separate unrelated ones with space.
4. Vary density across a sequence and repeat anchors (titles, folios, colours).
5. Format notes:
   - Poster: one dominant focus, headline readable from 2 m.
   - Slides: one idea per slide, 24 pt minimum, fixed title position.
   - Web: 12-column fluid grid, 8-pt spacing, phone/tablet/desktop breakpoints, 55-75 character lines.
   - Social: central safe zone, short large text.
   - Packaging: panel map, fold lines, legal and barcode zones.
   - Multi-page documents: facing spreads, gutter allowance, master pages, running heads, page numbers.

### Picture books

- Total pages in multiples of 8 (32, 40, 48); front matter counts inside the total. Page 1 is a right-hand page, so spreads are 2-3, 4-5, and so on; always review facing pairs.
- One story beat per spread: one idea, one image, one emotion. Roughly 15-60 words per spread for ages 3-8.
- Put reveals and questions on the right-hand page so the page turn builds suspense. Include at least one wordless spread at the emotional peak and a quiet spread before the ending.
- Keep text and faces at least 12-15 mm from the fold, outer margins at least 10-12 mm. Never let text or a face cross the fold.
- Place text in a calm area of the art; move the text block between spreads but keep font and size fixed. Use a pale wash behind text, not a hard box.
- Vary the shot (wide, medium, close) so consecutive spreads differ. Keep the focal subject off the fold. Left-to-right movement reads as progress, right-to-left as return.
- Story text 14-18 pt for ages 3-6; display face only for titles and sound words.

## 5. Illustration and fine art

- **Structure:** perspective with vanishing points, figures and animals built from simple forms, three rapid thumbnails per composition before final art.
- **Value:** plan lightest and darkest areas first; use light, core shadow and highlight to build volume; control hard, soft and lost edges to steer focus.
- **Colour script:** choose a palette per scene that conveys time of day and emotion; follow a story-wide arc (for example dark to light to dark). Warm gold and rose for comfort, cool blues and greens for night and worry.
- **Shape psychology:** sharp triangles for tension, soft circles for comfort, big shapes for power, small shapes for vulnerability, merged shapes for closeness.
- **Storytelling:** arrange characters, focal points and negative space to tell one clear story per frame; use visual metaphor and symbols for emotion without text.
- **Mixed media:** scan ink, watercolor washes and paper grain and composite them digitally; keep one grain and one line weight across pages.
- **Watercolor and nature style:** soft wet-in-wet edges, dry-brush for focus, blooms and granulation, limited palette, visible paper, no hard outlines.
- **Personal voice:** simplify reality, repeat shapes and colours, keep decisions consistent.

## 6. Production

- Print: CMYK check, 300 dpi, bleed, crop marks, embedded or outlined fonts, safe area, proof before final export. Choose a font licence that covers print.
- Screen: RGB, optimized exports, accessible contrast, scalable text.
- Specialty print (risograph, screenprint, packaging): limit colours, separate layers per ink, check overprint and registration.

## 7. Review checklist

Go in this order and give exact fixes:

1. Hierarchy: one clear focal point and clear text levels.
2. Grid and alignment; margins and safe zones respected; nothing in the fold or bleed zone.
3. Type: two families, scale followed, line length and leading right.
4. Colour: palette limits, contrast ratios, mood matches the story.
5. Texture and surface: one grain, consistent edges, no clash of flat and painted looks.
6. Rhythm: varied density, pacing, repeated anchors, page turns land on reveals.
7. Thumbnail test and greyscale test: readable at small size, values hold without colour.
8. Production readiness.

## 8. Practice drills (when teaching)

Three poster layouts on a strict grid; a text-heavy spread with two typefaces; four abstract shape-emotion compositions; twenty thumbnails; five grayscale value studies; a scanned-texture composite; a four-page zine with bleed; two contrasting direction boards for a fictional brand; a mini brand kit with logo, palette, type guide and two illustrations.

## Output format

For creation: state the style card (palette, fonts, texture, layout rules) in a few lines, then build. For critique: a short ranked list of problems with concrete fixes. For teaching: one skill, one exercise, one success check.
