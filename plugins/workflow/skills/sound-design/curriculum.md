# Curriculum

Use this when someone wants to learn rather than get a sound made. Ask once how many hours a week
they have and whether the aim is games, film or music; the default is games, at one milestone a
week. Teach one milestone at a time. The learner listens and judges, and you explain
and check their work with `measure-sound.sh`, since only they can hear it.

## Phase 1: synthesis

How sound is generated and shaped from nothing. Theory and recipes: `synthesis.md`.
Learn on [Ableton Learning Synths](https://learningsynths.ableton.com/), then build in
[Strudel](https://strudel.cc/).

| Week | Learn                                          | Do                                                      | Done when                                           |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | oscillators, low-pass and high-pass filters    | hear each waveform, then sweep a filter over a sawtooth | can name a waveform by ear                          |
| 2    | ADSR envelopes, LFOs                           | turn one note into a pluck, a swell and a wobble        | can explain why `sustain` is a level and not a time |
| 3    | additive, subtractive, wavetable, FM, granular | make the same note with each method                     | can say which method fits a brief, and why          |

**Project:** a bass, a lead melody and a sci-fi atmospheric pad, in a browser synthesizer only.
Check each spectrum: the bass in the low bands, the lead in the mid, the pad moving over time.

## Phase 2: editing

Moving from making sounds to cleaning and analysing recordings. Reference: `editing.md`.
Learn in [Audacity](https://support.audacityteam.org/).

| Week | Learn                                    | Do                                                      | Done when                                      |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 4    | destructive and non-destructive edits    | cut, reverse and fade a recording, then undo the choice | can say which of those steps can be taken back |
| 5    | amplitude envelopes, transients          | sharpen one hit and soften another by volume alone      | lead silence under 5 ms on the sharpened hit   |
| 6    | spectral analysis, hum and noise removal | find a hum in the spectrum and remove only that         | hum gone, voice level unchanged                |

**Project:** record a voice or a household sound, clean the background noise, then redesign it
into something robotic or monstrous that nobody could recognise. Compare before and after by
spectrogram and report the noise floor of each.

## Phase 3: foley and layering

Sourcing material, layering textures and arranging to a timeline. Reference:
`foley-and-layering.md`.

| Week | Learn                                   | Do                                                         | Done when                                                            |
| ---- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 7    | how sounds are labelled and licensed    | describe three Freesound uploads at all four levels        | can say which of them could ship, and why                            |
| 8    | sync to picture                         | foley a short walk on screen: feet, moves and one specific | every contact lands on or just after its frame                       |
| 9    | risers, sub-booms, multi-track layering | build a rise, a silence and a hit on one cut               | the riser ends before the hit, and the mix peaks at or below -1 dBTP |

**Project:** a 30-second silent clip, with rights to use it. Source CC0 sounds, record your own
foley, and build a multi-track sequence matching the visual tension, stems kept separate.
